### ONRN-PA-0001 — Hyperkalemia — ECG progression

- **Status:** Source verified
- **Enemy group:** `hyperkalemia-ecg-earliest-sign`
- **Author log:** Key and all four rationales checked against live-fetched Merck (3 topics) and OpenStax pages. Note: OpenStax Table 10.7 also lists U waves under hyperkalemia, which conflicts with Merck; that disputed detail is not keyed — U waves are used only as the hypokalemia distractor, supported by Merck.
- **Sources:**
  - Merck Manual Professional Edition — [Hyperkalemia](https://www.merckmanuals.com/professional/nephrology/electrolyte-disorders/hyperkalemia) § Diagnosis of Hyperkalemia — ECG (source_date: Last updated Jun 2025; accessed 2026-11-25)
    - Evidence note: ECG changes often visible when K > 5.5 mmol/L; tall, symmetric, peaked T waves initially; >6.5 mmol/L widened QRS, loss of P wave; finally sine wave then VF/asystole; QT shortening.
  - OpenStax — Medical-Surgical Nursing — [10.3 Electrolyte Imbalance (Table 10.7 Hypokalemia and Hyperkalemia)](https://openstax.org/books/medical-surgical-nursing/pages/10-3-electrolyte-imbalance) § Potassium Imbalances — Table 10.7 (source_date: Citation date Oct 16, 2024; accessed 2026-11-25)
    - Evidence note: Hyperkalemia clinical manifestations: tall, peaked T waves, widened QRS complex, prolonged PR interval.
  - Merck Manual Professional Edition — [Hypokalemia](https://www.merckmanuals.com/professional/nephrology/electrolyte-disorders/hypokalemia) § Diagnosis of Hypokalemia — ECG (source_date: Last updated Dec 2025; accessed 2026-11-25)
    - Evidence note: Hypokalemia causes ST sagging, T-wave depression and U-wave elevation; T wave shrinks and U wave enlarges as it worsens.
  - Merck Manual Professional Edition — [Hypocalcemia](https://www.merckmanuals.com/professional/nephrology/electrolyte-disorders/hypocalcemia) § Diagnosis of Hypocalcemia (source_date: Last updated Jun 2025; accessed 2026-11-25)
    - Evidence note: Severe hypocalcemia typically shows prolongation of the QTc and ST intervals.

    - Evidence note: Mild-moderate: 15 g carbohydrate, preferably glucose or sucrose tablets/solution; retest in 15 min, re-treat if <4.0 mmol/L; avoid overtreatment; snack with 15 g carb + protein if meal >1 h away; unconscious without IV: glucagon 1 mg SC/IM.
