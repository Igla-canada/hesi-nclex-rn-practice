### ONRN-RRP-0002 — Transfusion reaction — stop infusion and keep IV open with saline

- **Status:** Source verified
- **Enemy group:** `transfusion-reaction-stop-keep-iv-saline`
- **Author log:** Key and all rationales checked against live-fetched Merck (Oct 2026) and OpenStax Clinical Nursing Skills 13.4. Localized urticaria-only exception in Merck is not tested.
- **Sources:**
  - Merck Manual Professional Edition — [Complications of Transfusion](https://www.merckmanuals.com/professional/hematology/transfusion-medicine/complications-of-transfusion) § Complications of Transfusion — early recognition (source_date: Last updated Oct 2026; accessed 2026-11-25)
    - Evidence note: If chills, fever, dyspnea, flank pain (other than localized urticaria/itching) occur, stop transfusion immediately; keep IV open with normal saline; send remainder of product for investigation; do not restart the unit.
  - OpenStax — Clinical Nursing Skills — [13.4 Blood Transfusions](https://openstax.org/books/clinical-nursing-skills/pages/13-4-blood-transfusions) § Transfusion Reactions; Managing a Blood or Blood Product Transfusion Reaction (source_date: Citation date Jun 26, 2024; accessed 2026-11-25)
    - Evidence note: Nurses must recognize transfusion reactions and stop the transfusion; management includes maintaining IV access and following reaction protocols with blood bank notification.

### ONRN-PA-0004 — Hypovolemic shock — early compensatory tachycardia

- **Status:** Source verified
- **Enemy group:** `hypovolemic-shock-early-compensatory-tachycardia`
- **Author log:** Keyed early compensation (tachycardia with preserved BP) confirmed in Merck Shock and OpenStax 23.2. Neurogenic contrast from standard shock classification in Merck.
- **Sources:**
  - Merck Manual Professional Edition — [Shock](https://www.merckmanuals.com/professional/critical-care-medicine/shock-and-fluid-resuscitation/shock) § Pathophysiology of Shock; Etiology — Hypovolemic shock; Compensation (source_date: Last updated Jul 2026; accessed 2026-11-25)
    - Evidence note: Symptoms include tachycardia, hypotension, oliguria; hypotension common but not required — some patients normotensive early; hypovolemia triggers sympathetic tachycardia and vasoconstriction.
  - OpenStax — Medical-Surgical Nursing — [23.2 Hypovolemic Shock](https://openstax.org/books/medical-surgical-nursing/pages/23-2-hypovolemic-shock) § Pathophysiology of Hypovolemic Shock; Clinical Manifestations (source_date: Citation date Oct 16, 2024; accessed 2026-11-25)
    - Evidence note: Early hypovolemic shock: SNS raises heart rate/contractility; BP normal to slightly elevated early; later hypotension with tachycardia; classic signs include tachycardia, cool clammy skin, anxiety.
